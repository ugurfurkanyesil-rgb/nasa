# NASA tools and design inspirations

Martian Map is a planning tool for one 2 km × 2 km site at a time. Several NASA tools shaped how it looks and works. This page lists each one, what it actually is, and which design decision in Martian Map came from it. Where a tool gave no concrete design decision, the page says so.

None of these tools supplies data to Martian Map. All the Mars data comes from the mission products listed in [DATA_PROVENANCE.md](DATA_PROVENANCE.md).

| Tool | What it is | Design decision taken in Martian Map | What it is **not** |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NASA Mars Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/mars/) | JPL's browser portal for Mars. You can stack and blend data layers, measure distances, draw elevation profiles, and run slope and sun-angle analysis. | (1) **Layers drape on the terrain with an opacity slider** (*Map Layers → DRAPE ON 3D TERRAIN*). (2) **Elevation profile** of every planned route, with the stops numbered, on the route card and the cuff card. (3) **Slope and lighting** worked out for the EVA's own date and time instead of a fixed sun. | Mars Trek covers the whole planet at regional scale. Martian Map plans a single Marswalk on a 4 m grid. |
| [NASA Moon Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/moon/) | The lunar portal in the same family, used for Artemis mission planning. Its tools include elevation profiles, solar altitude and azimuth, and distance measurement. | **Solar altitude and azimuth along the route.** Martian Map computes the sun (Mars24) for the moment each point is walked and casts terrain shadows. This is the same kind of analysis, applied to a Mars EVA. | The Moon simulator at `?site=moon` is a procedural test terrain. It does **not** use Moon Trek data and is not a real lunar location. |
| [NASA Solar System Treks](https://trek.nasa.gov/) | The project that runs the Trek portals (Moon, Mars, Vesta, Ceres, Titan, Icy Moons, Mercury …) with one shared interface. | **One interface and one pipeline for every site.** Jezero and Mount Sharp come out of the same pipeline (`pipeline/build_*.py`) and share one interface, with a site switcher at the top left. | No other body's data is used. |
| [NASA Worldview](https://worldview.earthdata.nasa.gov/) | An Earth satellite-imagery browser with a timeline and a swipe **comparison mode**. | (1) **COMPARE LAYERS** is a draggable swipe between two layers of a site, for example the HiRISE image against CRISM minerals or the hazard map. (2) **The date drives the data.** The EVA date and time set the sun, the temperature climatology and the DONKI lookup. | Worldview shows **Earth** data only. It supplies no Mars data and none is claimed. |
| [NASA's Eyes on the Solar System](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/) | A real-time 3D view of planets and spacecraft where you can run time forwards or backwards. | **A mission clock you can scrub and speed up.** EVA playback runs at ×1 … ×600 with pause and abort. The crew positions come from the planner's own timeline. | Eyes shows orbital and system-scale motion. It does not map the surface or plan EVAs. |
| [NASA's Eyes on Exoplanets](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/exo/) | A 3D visualisation of planetary systems around other stars, built on the NASA Exoplanet Archive. One feature compares a system with our own. | **Compare with something familiar.** The radiation panel shows Mars next to Earth, the ISS and the Moon (measured daily dose, log scale) and gives each EVA's dose as "days of Earth background". This is a loose parallel only. | It is **not** a surface-exploration map and holds no Mars surface data. Nothing else in Martian Map comes from it. |
| Apollo 15–17 cuff checklists and traverse planning data | Small wrist-mounted checklists and photo maps with station times. The Apollo 17 traverse plan kept a 30-minute life-support reserve on every walk-back. | (1) **PRINT CUFF CARD** produces one A4 page: a photo map, then station arrival, stay, BACK and LEAVE BY times in Apollo-style *h+mm*. (2) **The 30-minute reserve** is subtracted from the Mars-conservative EVA budget, and a margin below it makes a plan CONDITIONAL under the lunar limits. | The Apollo checklists are lunar documents. Their walking values are not presented as Mars standards. |
| Rover route and science-planning visualisations: *Curiosity's proposed path up Mount Sharp* ([PIA23179](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/curiositys-proposed-path-up-mount-sharp/)) and its 2023 sequel (PIA26042) | Orbital images with the rover's route drawn as a pale line and the regions it crosses labelled. | **…'S ROUTE & PLACES**: the rover's localised traverse through each site (MMGIS waypoints), drawn as a pale line on the map and in 3D. The planned Marswalk is drawn over it, so the crew can see where a rover has already been. The same rover positions decide which hazard-map cells have **ground truth**. | Region labels at Gale are this project's derived levels, not NASA's mapped units (the app says so). |

## Decisions that came from the challenge brief, not from a tool

- **Choosing a science objective before planning.** The brief asks crews to identify a science objective and a location. The objective selector feeds the planner's scoring directly (see [README](../README.md#mission-objectives)).
- **Separating measured, orbital, derived, transferred, modelled and simulated data.** The brief stresses astronaut safety. A transferred or derived value presented as a local measurement would be a safety error, so the category is shown on every layer and every stop card.
